How to play on a fall outing

Take this bingo outdoors on a walk, or play it at a school harvest party. It works as a spotting game and as a quiet seated game, and the sights give you plenty to talk about.

  1. Give each person a card, a pencil and a small paper bag for collecting leaves or acorns.
  2. Head outside and cover squares as you find them, or call them out for a seated game.
  3. Players may collect safe items such as fallen leaves, and leave living plants and nuts for wildlife.
  4. Return to base when someone has a row, and share what you saw.
  5. Press the best leaves between book pages for a craft later.

Three seasonal ideas

  • Orchard edition. Print a card with apple varieties, a ladder, a basket and a farm cat. Children cross squares as they pick.
  • Colors of fall. Each square asks for a color found in a leaf, like crimson, gold and brown. Children match real leaves to the color words.
  • Cozy night in. Play with a blanket and a mug on a rainy day, with squares such as soup, a sweater and a fire in the grate.

Tips for autumn adventures

Dress for changing weather. Fall afternoons can be warm in the sun and cold in the shade, and wet leaves are slippery on paths.

Talk about why leaves change color, if the group is curious. A short explanation from a nature guide helps children connect the squares to what they see.

Keep a few blank squares for surprises. Children love to add their own sighting on the spot.

Printing for outdoor use

Use a clipboard and a pencil, because pens skip on cool, damp paper. Print on slightly thicker paper if you expect drizzle, and fold the card into a pocket.

Orange, tan or cream paper suits the season, and ink saver keeps the colors warm instead of heavy.

FAQ

Can I use fall bingo in a classroom?

Yes. Use it with a seasonal reading unit or a nature walk on school grounds. Print enough for each pair of students.

What about places without fall colors?

Edit the list. Swap in squares like first rain, cooler air or a harvest festival that fit your region.

Is this the same as Halloween bingo?

No. Fall bingo covers the whole season, while Halloween has its own page with spooky items.
