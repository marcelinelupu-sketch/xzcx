How to play during the first week

On day one, children are looking around the room and waiting to see who is nice. A bingo card gives them a reason to look, talk and settle. It works best during a quiet block, such as after morning meeting or before dismissal.

  1. Hand out cards face down and let everyone turn them over together.
  2. Read each square out loud, then ask students to find one in the room or tell a neighbor about it.
  3. Students cover each match with a counter or a crayon dot.
  4. The first student with a full row stands up and names three squares they covered.
  5. Collect the cards and display them on a bulletin board titled First Week Facts.

Three classroom variations

  • Find a friend. Each student finds a classmate who matches a square, such as someone who rode the bus, and gets a signature in the box. It turns the game into a gentle mixer.
  • Tour bingo. Walk the building in small groups, and have students cover squares like the library corner or the gym as they pass them.
  • Parent night edition. At open house, parents play with the card and learn about the day, such as where the lunch line starts and what the bell sounds like.

Tips for teachers

Choose words that every child can read, or read each word aloud for kindergarteners. Nobody should feel behind on day one.

Mark a few squares as teacher squares, like a smile or a story. These give you a quick and friendly way to join the game without leading it.

Anxious students often feel better when there is a task. Offer the card to a child who clings at the door and let them mark the squares as they find them.

Printing for the classroom

Print on regular copy paper, one card per child, and use two per page to cut costs. Choose ink saver for a clean look. Sheets can be stapled inside a folder to save for later.

If you reuse cards across several sections, put them in sheet protectors and use dry-erase markers. A quick wipe resets each one.

FAQ

Is this good for the very first day?

Yes. It keeps students busy while you handle paperwork and routines, and nothing on the card requires prior knowledge.

Can I use it as an icebreaker with older students?

Absolutely. Trim the list to items suited for their grade and ask students to find classmates who match.

How many cards can I print at once?

Up to 30 unique cards, enough for a typical class. Use the seed link to reprint the same set later.
