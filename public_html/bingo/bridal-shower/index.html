How to run bridal shower bingo

Bridal showers have a long middle stretch when the guest of honor opens one present after another and everyone else watches. Bingo gives the room a shared task and keeps conversation flowing, especially when guests come from different parts of the bride's life and do not all know each other.

  1. Give every guest a card and a pen as they sit down after lunch or brunch.
  2. Explain that gifts and party moments both count, so a champagne flute and a happy tear are equal.
  3. As the bride opens each present, the guests mark any matching square.
  4. A guest who completes a line says "Bingo" and the bride, or the maid of honor, checks it before a prize goes out.
  5. Play several rounds: one line, then two lines, then a full card for the grand prize.

Assign a friend to jot the presents down on the caller checklist. It doubles as a gift record for the thank you notes, which the bride will be glad to have the next week.

Ways to change the game

The nobody says it version

Print cards with phrases the bride is sure to say or hear, such as "It's so pretty", "You shouldn't have" and "Where did you find this?". Guests mark a square each time they hear the phrase. It makes the room laugh and works even for guests who miss the present.

Love story bingo

Write memories from the couple's history in the squares, like where they met, who proposed or the first trip together. Guests who know the answers mark them as the host reads clues aloud.

Sip and mark

For a champagne brunch, set each prize round to a toast. Everyone raises a glass when someone calls bingo, and the winner picks the next round's pattern.

Host tips that help

Seat the maid of honor next to the bride, so she can pass the presents and say the name of each giver out loud. That helps guests who are playing bingo to keep track of what was just opened.

Pick prizes that suit the setting: a mini candle, a tea sampler or a bottle of nail polish. Tie a ribbon in the wedding colors around each prize and set them on the gift table.

A short game feels better than a long one. If the present pile is large, call bingo on single lines only, otherwise the room runs out of patience before the last box.

Planning the game around the schedule

Slot bingo in right after the meal, once plates are cleared and before dessert comes out. At that point the room is settled, everyone can see the bride, and nobody is rushing to leave. If you also have a trivia game or a decorating station planned, play bingo during the gifts and save the others for the first half of the party.

Tell guests at the start that the game is optional and relaxed. Some will want to compete for the prize, others will mark squares between conversations, and both styles work.

Printing for a shower

Printing on a soft cream or blush paper makes the cards feel part of the table setting. Switch on ink saver so the grid stays crisp and light, and print one test page to check how the color looks beside your invitations.

If you are assembling place settings, print two cards per page and trim them to half sheets. A thin ribbon or a clothespin holds the card and a pen together at each seat.

FAQ

How many people can play bridal shower bingo?

Any number up to 30 on the first print, and you can generate another set for a bigger party. Since each card is shuffled from 50 squares, duplicates are very unlikely.

What should I do if the bride has few gifts?

Use a mix of moments and gifts. Squares such as a toast, a happy tear or a piece of marriage advice will fill even when only a few boxes are opened.

Can the guest list talk be part of the game?

Yes. Wedding date, venue and dress talk squares are already in the list. Remove any topics the bride would rather not discuss.

Can I turn the cards into a wedding day game too?

You can. Swap out gift squares for ceremony and reception moments, or use the wedding reception bingo page for that.
