How to play at the campsite

Camping bingo works best as a slow game that unfolds over a whole weekend. Pass out cards when you arrive and keep them near the cooler or inside the tent so people can mark squares between hikes, meals and chores.

  1. Give each camper a card and a stub of pencil, which survives dew better than a pen.
  2. Campers cover a square whenever they see, hear or use the item, so chopping firewood counts as soon as it happens.
  3. Ask for a witness on wildlife squares, because a chipmunk is easier to doubt than a tent stake.
  4. A full row earns a small camp job off the list, such as skipping dish duty.
  5. At the last evening campfire, tally up the cards and read the rarest squares aloud.

Three variations around the fire

  • Night sky edition. Make a second sheet with only sounds and sights from after dark, like an owl, a star shower and crickets. It gives restless kids a reason to stay by the fire.
  • Camp chore bingo. Mix chores into the list, such as hauling water or sweeping the tent, and use the card as a fair way to hand out work.
  • Silent round. For a morning bird walk, nobody speaks and everyone covers squares quietly. Compare cards afterward.

Tips for leaders and parents

Keep a small bin of tiny prizes. Pinecone ornaments, bandanas or an extra marshmallow all feel like trophies after a cold morning.

Camp cooking makes some squares easy to complete and others dependent on weather. Plan a backup list for wet days so the game does not stall when the fire will not light.

If you lead a scout troop or group, reward teamwork. A square can be covered only if two campers sign it, which encourages kids to share finds.

Printing for the outdoors

A paper card gets damp in a tent, so slip it into a zip-top bag or print on a heavier cardstock. Dry-erase markers work well through a page protector and wipe clean for the next game.

Print in black and white with ink saver turned on. The grid stays readable in lantern light, and you will not mind if a card ends up smudged by a s'more.

FAQ

Can camping bingo be played without a campfire?

Yes. Delete the fire squares and add things like a clothesline, a bird at the feeder or a tree stump so that backyard or cabin stays work just as well.

How many cards do I need for a scout troop?

Print one per scout plus a few spares. If the group is large, print four per page and cut them apart to save paper.

What is a good prize for camp bingo?

Small, practical rewards go over best, like a glow stick, a trail snack or first pick of the sleeping spot on the next trip.
