How to play for small children

Farm bingo is often a child's first game with rules, so go slowly. Sit on the floor in a circle, hand out cards and treat each call as a tiny story.

  1. Give each child a card and a handful of buttons, dried beans or small blocks.
  2. The caller holds up a picture or says an animal name. Making the animal's sound is half the fun.
  3. Children who find the square put a marker on it.
  4. When a child has covered a whole row, everyone says moo together.
  5. Swap the caller so each child gets a turn at making sounds.

Three more ways to play

  • Sound only. The caller makes the noise and children guess the animal before covering the square.
  • Baby and parent match. Call a baby, such as a lamb, and players cover the grown-up animal, sheep, instead.
  • Farm walk. On a visit to a petting farm, children cover the squares as they actually meet each animal.

Tips for preschool and home

Keep games short, around ten minutes. If attention fades, stop on a high note and try again after snack.

Pair the cards with a picture book about farm life. After reading, children recognize more squares, which builds confidence.

For children who are not yet reading, write the word with a small drawing beside it. Parents can draw simple shapes on the free square.

Printing for tiny hands

Print one big card per page for toddlers, since small squares are hard to cover. Heavy paper stands up to wet fingers.

Cut a second set of words into call cards, and glue a picture on each one. Laminate if you plan to reuse them in the next unit.

FAQ

What size grid should I use for toddlers?

A three by three grid with large print is best for ages two to four. Older children can handle four by four or five by five.

Does the list include baby animals?

Yes. Calves, lambs, kid goats, foals and chicks all appear so children learn the names of young animals too.

Can I use it at a farm visit?

Yes. Print the card before you leave and let children cover squares as they see each animal or tool.
