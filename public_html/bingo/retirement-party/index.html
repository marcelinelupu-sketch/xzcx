How to play at a retirement send-off

The program at a retirement party usually involves several speeches and a slideshow. Bingo keeps coworkers engaged and adds a friendly laugh, especially when the retiree knows what is coming and plays along.

  1. Place a card on every chair in the room or hand one out with the plate of food.
  2. Announce that squares can be marked when they happen, whether it is a funny story or a gift.
  3. The retiree keeps a card too and can mark off the moments they notice.
  4. The first person to fill a row calls it out and gets a small prize presented by the retiree.
  5. Continue until the cake is cut, and then call a final blackout round before everyone leaves.

Three variations

  • Inside joke bingo. Write in-jokes, nicknames and catchphrases of the retiree in the squares. People who worked with them for years know them best.
  • Plan predictor. Fill the squares with what the retiree might do first, like travel, golf or sleeping in, then let guests guess before the speech.
  • Remote retirement. For colleagues joining on a video call, email the PDF in advance and have everyone mark squares as speakers take the screen.

Tips for organizers

Check with the retiree's family about topics to avoid. A good rule is that every square should make the retiree smile, not squirm.

Make the prize modest and thoughtful, like a coffee voucher or a box of good tea. The last prize can go to the retiree.

If the group is large, hand out cards in advance with the invitation, so people can think about their squares before they arrive.

Printing for the office

Most offices have a laser printer, which handles the grid well. Print two cards per page and cut them on the paper trimmer for a stack of half sheets.

Using black and white mode saves toner, and a sheet of gold paper cut into a border adds a festive touch. Print a single large card as a poster to display in the breakroom.

FAQ

Is this appropriate for a workplace?

Yes, as long as the squares are kind. The list sticks to light, friendly topics. Review it before printing and remove anything that does not fit your office.

How many cards should I print?

One per guest, plus some spare ones. If the guest list is not final, print twenty and run another batch the day before.

Can the retiree help make the card?

It is a fun idea. Ask them for a few favorite sayings or office habits, and add them to the list before generating.
