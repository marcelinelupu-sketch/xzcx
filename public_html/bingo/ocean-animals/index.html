How to play with a marine theme

Ocean animals bingo works well as a review game after a few days of study. Because the animals are so varied, a teacher can end the game by sorting the squares into groups like fish, mammals and invertebrates.

  1. Give each student a card and a handful of blue paper squares or counters, which look like water on the grid.
  2. The caller picks a name from a cup, reads it aloud and adds one fact, such as where it lives or what it eats.
  3. Players who have the animal cover the square.
  4. A full line wins, but the winner must name the animals in that line before collecting the prize.
  5. Clear the cards and play again with a new caller.

Three ways to dive deeper

  • Clue calling. Instead of reading the name, give a clue such as eight arms and three hearts, so players must figure out that it is an octopus.
  • Depth round. Group squares by zone, sunlit, twilight or deep sea, and play separate games for each zone.
  • Aquarium scavenger. At an aquarium, cover squares by actually seeing the animal behind the glass and note the tank on the back.

Tips for teachers and hosts

Pick a few squares as anchors for facts. Orcas are dolphins, not whales, and seahorse fathers carry the eggs. Children remember details attached to a game.

Have a world map nearby. Pointing out where each animal lives adds geography without lengthening the game.

If students struggle with spelling, project the word list on a screen so they can see each name while the caller reads it.

Printing for splash-friendly play

Print on pale blue paper for a watery feel and use ink saver mode to keep colored paper readable. If cards will be handled at a pool party, laminate them or slide them into plastic sleeves.

Teachers can print two per page and cut the sheet in half to save paper, then collect the cards at the end for reuse next year.

FAQ

Which ocean animals are on the list?

There are forty, including fish, whales, sharks, shellfish and sea birds. You can edit the box above to add local species.

Is this good for kindergarten?

Yes with a three by three grid and picture support. Older kids can use a five by five grid and write one fact per found animal.

Can I use it at an aquarium?

Yes. Print before you go, trim the list to what the aquarium shows, and let children cover squares in front of the tanks.
