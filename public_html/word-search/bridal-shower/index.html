How to run it at the shower

This game works at brunches, tea parties and backyard gatherings because it needs only paper and a pen.

  1. Put a copy at each place setting before guests arrive.
  2. Announce that the first to circle all twenty words wins a small prize.
  3. Let the bride read the list of words aloud when the winner finishes.
  4. Ask each guest to share a wish for the couple using one of the words.

If the party is formal, set the puzzle beside a place card so it feels part of the table rather than an interruption.

Ways to make it your own

  • Couple's story. Replace five words with places, songs or nicknames that matter to the couple.
  • Advice cards. After the puzzle, pass out cards where guests write a short bit of marriage advice using a word from the grid.
  • Rapid round. Set a three-minute timer and see who finds the most words, instead of all of them.

Tips for hosts and maids of honor

Keep the prizes small and fun, such as a candle or a mini bottle of lotion. Large stakes make a quiet game feel competitive.

Make sure guests who prefer not to play have something else to do, like decorating a card for the couple.

Tell guests ahead that a puzzle will be part of the afternoon, so people who enjoy word games come ready to play.

Printing for an elegant table

Cream or blush cardstock gives the page a stationery feel. If you want it to match your invitations, print on the same paper stock.

Reduce the size of the word list or turn it off for a harder puzzle, then offer the list to only those who ask.

FAQ

Can I put the bride's name in the puzzle?

Yes. Add her name and the groom's name to the list, then press Generate. Both are hidden in the grid along with the wedding words.

How many puzzles should I print?

One for each guest plus a few spares is enough. The multiple-puzzle setting gives each table different layouts.

Is the puzzle suitable for a mixed-age crowd?

Yes. The words are all familiar, so guests from teens to grandparents can join in.
