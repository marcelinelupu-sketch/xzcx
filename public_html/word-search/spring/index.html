How to use this word search for a spring unit

Spring words connect easily to science, from seeds and weather to animal life. This puzzle is a quiet bridge between the indoor lessons of winter and the outdoor lessons to come.

  1. Before solving, ask children to name signs of spring they have already noticed, then check how many appear in the list.
  2. Have them solve the puzzle and put a star by each word that matches something they saw this week.
  3. Take the sheet outside and add words they spot that are not on the list.
  4. Back inside, write three sentences using the new words.

You can tie the puzzle to citizen science. Programs like Nature's Notebook invite families and classes to record when plants bud and leaf out in their area.

Three ideas to extend the puzzle

  • Weather diary. Children record the weather for a week using words from the puzzle such as breeze, puddle and rainbow.
  • Seed to sprout. Plant beans in clear cups and label each stage with vocabulary words from the puzzle.
  • Spring scavenger hunt. Turn the words into an outdoor checklist and see who finds the most.

Tips for teachers and parents

Early spring can mean mud and cold, and later spring can mean windows open and wandering minds. Short puzzles are good for both.

Ask children to add one word of their own to the list, and make a puzzle for the whole class from their suggestions.

Printing tips for the season

For outdoor classrooms, print on regular paper and tuck it in a clipboard with a clip. Pencil works better than marker if the day is damp.

The medium 15 by 15 grid suits second grade and up. Use the smaller size for younger children.
