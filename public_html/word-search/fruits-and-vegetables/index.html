How to play at the lunch table or in class

Produce words are easy for most children to read because they see them on labels and in the cart. This makes a nice confidence-builder puzzle.

  1. Give every child a puzzle and a crayon.
  2. Find all the fruits and circle them in red.
  3. Find all the vegetables and circle them in green.
  4. Count how many of each you found and compare.

Younger children can be given just the fruit half of the list, then return for the vegetables on another day.

Tasty variations

  • Taste test. Buy four items from the list, slice them, and let children rate each from one to five after finding the words.
  • Color rainbow. Group the words by color on a paper rainbow with one band per color.
  • Grow it. Ask which items grow underground, on a vine, on a tree or on a bush, and sort them that way.

Tips for picky eaters and busy parents

Puzzles are a pressure-free way to talk about vegetables. Do not push anyone to eat anything, just let the words become familiar.

If you shop together, let a child bring the circled list to the store and find a new item to try.

A real produce basket on the table makes the words feel solid. Let children hold a pepper or a pear while they search for its name.

Printing for the kitchen

Put a copy on the fridge with a magnet and let children circle a word each time they eat that food during the week. A sheet slipped in a plastic page protector wipes clean after sticky fingers.

Pastel orange or green paper looks bright, and black ink still reads clearly on it.

FAQ

Is a tomato a fruit or a vegetable in this list?

Botanically it is a fruit, but cooks treat it as a vegetable, and so does this list. It makes a fun question to discuss.

Can I use this for a nutrition lesson?

Yes. Pair it with a plate diagram from a reliable source such as MyPlate at myplate.gov to talk about balanced meals.

Are there seasonal items I can swap in?

Edit the list freely. Many families replace a few words with whatever is at the local farmers market.
