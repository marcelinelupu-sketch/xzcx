How to play on Halloween

Halloween is the one night of the year when children are out in the dark and delighted about it. A scavenger hunt helps them notice the decorations, and for the shyest ones gives a reason to keep walking between houses.

  1. Choose where to play: trick-or-treat night with a grown-up, a school party room, or the front yard with decorations you set up.
  2. Give each child a sheet, a pencil and a flashlight for ticking, and keep a spare for those who lose theirs.
  3. Walk the route with an adult and tick items as you go. Costumes count, but only if the wearer is happy to be pointed out.
  4. Wait for the treats to be collected before the sorting begins, then count up who found the most.
  5. Share the funniest find at the end of the night.

Spooky variations

  • Party room hunt. Hide paper bats, plastic spiders and paper ghosts in a room, and give each child a list of what to spot. No walking required.
  • Trick-or-treat tally. Add a bar and points for each decoration you see on the route, then compare totals as treats get sorted.
  • Friendly monster mash. Add a line to each item where children write what the creature might say. Read them out as the evening winds down.

Tips for a spooky night

Keep the mood playful. A few children will be nervous of the dark, so avoid pushing them toward the scariest decoration.

Add glow bracelets so adults can keep a head count in the dark.

Remove any item you think may upset a particular child, such as skeletons, and replace it with something friendly like a pumpkin.

Safety on Halloween night

Children should go with an adult or in a group with an adult nearby, carry a flashlight and wear reflective strips or light colors. Cross streets at corners, stay on the sidewalk and keep to well-lit streets.

Only visit houses with a light on, never go inside a stranger's home, and check treats with an adult before eating, especially if there are allergies. Choose costumes that do not block vision or trip a child.

Printing for the evening

Print on orange or black paper if you have it, but white is easier to read by flashlight. Use cardstock for a trick-or-treat bag insert, or tape the sheet to the back of a clipboard. Ink saver mode keeps the dark frog stamp light, and a pencil is easier to tick with than a pen when the paper gets damp.

FAQ

Is the Halloween list scary for young children?

It is meant to be playful: ghosts, pumpkins, cats and bats. You can remove any item that might upset a specific child.

Can I use it at a school Halloween party?

Yes. Print a class set, hide paper decorations around the room and let children search in teams. It works well when costumes make a regular lesson hard.

How do I include points?

Points appear after a bar in the list. Rare finds such as a zombie hand carry more points. Delete the numbers if you want a no-scoring game.
