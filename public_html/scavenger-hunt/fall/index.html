How to go on a fall hunt

Autumn does the decorating for you: the colors, textures and smells are all outside. A fall hunt simply asks children to slow down and notice them, and a big pile of leaves is the reward at the end.

  1. Pick a place with trees, such as a park, an orchard, a pumpkin patch or your own street, and a clear fall day.
  2. Hand out a sheet to each child, with pencils on a clipboard, a small bag or bucket and a jacket.
  3. Walk the route together. Children tick items as they spot them, and the leaf colors can be matched to real leaves on the ground.
  4. Collect fallen leaves only where it is allowed, and choose just one per child to keep.
  5. Press the leaves between heavy books at home and use them for a collage later.

Autumn variations

  • Leaf color match. Take paint swatches in autumn shades and find a leaf to match each one.
  • Pumpkin patch version. Add a few items to the list, such as the roundest pumpkin and the tallest one, and play at a farm.
  • Sounds of fall. Have children tick items they hear, like leaves crunching, wind in branches or geese calling.

Tips for leaf lovers

Go after a dry spell, so the leaves crunch and the paths are firm. Wet leaves are slippery.

Keep a paper bag for collected leaves. Plastic traps moisture and makes them mold quickly.

Bring a snack of apple slices or cider donuts for a mid-hunt reward.

Safety on fall outings

Stay together and watch for slippery leaf piles on steps and paths, since wet leaves hide uneven ground. Look but do not taste wild berries, mushrooms and nuts you cannot identify, and leave nests and animal homes alone.

Dress in layers and bright colors during hunting season in areas where hunting is allowed, and ask local rangers about any trail closures.

Printing for crisp weather

Print on cardstock in warm paper colors such as cream or pale orange. Clear checklist layout works well when hands are chilly and gloves make small boxes hard to tick. Pencil works better than pens in damp weather, and a clear sleeve protects the sheet from drizzle.

FAQ

Can I collect leaves on the hunt?

Where it is allowed, a single leaf per child is a lovely souvenir. In parks and nature reserves, take only what has fallen on the ground, or just photograph the finds.

What if my trees are evergreens?

Swap the leaf items for pine cones, needles, moss and bark, or take a trip to a park with deciduous trees.

Is this suitable for a school field day?

Yes. Print a class set and send teams into the school grounds with an adult each. It is a simple way to link a science lesson on seasons to something real.
