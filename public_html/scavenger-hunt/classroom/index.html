How to run a classroom scavenger hunt

A classroom hunt works as a tour of the room and the people in it. On the first days of school, it replaces a long lecture on where things live with a game children remember.

  1. Before class, check the list against your room and cross out or add items.
  2. Set expectations: walking feet, quiet voices and no opening of cupboards without asking.
  3. Divide students into small teams of three or four, with a mix of confident and shy children.
  4. Give a time limit of ten to fifteen minutes and a signal for stopping, such as a chime.
  5. Review the answers as a class, using the finds to reinforce routines like where to hang coats and where to hand in homework.

Classroom variations

  • Whole-school tour. Extend the list to the hall, library and office, with a staff member walking along with each group.
  • Silent search. Students tick items without talking, and the first team to finish quietly wins. It builds calm and focus.
  • Mystery message. Place a letter beside each item and let teams collect the letters to unscramble a classroom motto.

Tips for teachers

Use the shuffle option to prevent crowding. Teams then head to different corners and the room stays calmer.

Pair children carefully. A hunt is a great chance to mix students who have not worked together before.

Keep a few items on the list that need conversation, like the birthday match, so students get to know each other.

Moving around the building

Walking feet only, and teams should stay within the areas you have marked. Any item outside the classroom needs an adult escort. Keep the aisles clear of backpacks and chairs, and have students leave cupboards and desks closed unless you invite them to look.

Printing for a full class

Choose the number of copies to match your class or number of teams and print on regular paper. The sheets are light on ink, so a class set costs very little. For reuse, laminate a set and use dry erase markers, or slip sheets into plastic pockets that you can wipe clean at the end of each lesson.

FAQ

What grade levels is the classroom hunt for?

Kindergarten to grade 5 works well. Read the list aloud for early readers, or give each team a helper from an older class.

How long does it take?

Plan 15 to 25 minutes with a short debrief. Shorten it by removing items outside the classroom.

Can I use it on the first day of school?

Yes, it is a good first-week activity. Students move, talk to each other and learn where things are in a low-pressure way.
